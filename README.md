## 🎯 About the Project

This repository documents how **Team 47 (Six Seven Co. Ltd.)** won **Champion** in the **UniExLab 80-Day Real-World Business Simulation Competition**.

Over 80 in-game days (about one hour of real play), our team ran **FreshSqueeze Sdn Bhd**, a Malaysian juice company selling Carrot, Mango and Coconut juice through its own retail stores and B2B wholesale deals, while the market turned sharply against us.

This repo contains our results, decisions, charts, and lessons learned.

## 🌪️ The Challenge: "Weathering the Storm"

The graded round was much harsher than the warm-up:

| Pressure | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| 📈 **Inflation spike** | Input costs +20–30%, demand −10–15%, customers +20% more price-sensitive (15–25 days) |
| 🌊 **Natural disaster** | Costs +25–40% and procurement unavailable for 4–7 days |
| 🏬 **Very high store rent** | Heavy fixed cost on all stores |
| 💸 **Thin cash cushion** | RM 5M cash, overdraft halved to RM 2.5M |
| ⚠️ **Risk limit** | Max 50 delinquent payouts |
| 🤝 **B2B lifeline** | \~40,000 units of demand at 15–25% margin, won by competitive bidding against rival teams |

| --- | --- |
| **Sales** | Retail Sales · B2B Sales · Stock Out · Revenue |
| **Finance** | Cash in Bank · Gross Profit · Net Profit · Operating Expenses |

## 🧠 Our Strategy

### 🏪 Retail

- Launched with **4 stores** in high-population areas: **Ipoh, Alor Setar, Johor Bahru, Kuala Lumpur**.
- Expanded to **7 stores** once financially stable.
- Sourced from both our own warehouse and third-party vendors to avoid stockouts.
- When vendors delivered late, we switched to supplying from the local warehouse, which kept stockout levels healthy.
- Ran continuous social media campaigns (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Google).

### 🏭 Warehouse & Production

- Started small to learn production, including testing production ratios.
- Secured a **RM 80M loan** to scale manufacturing.
- Expanded the warehouse to **3,000 sq ft** with **4 production machines**.
- When competitors undercut on price, we scaled production to **lower per-unit cost** and stay competitive.
- Bought raw materials when market prices were low.
- Prioritised **Deal Orders**, which carried higher margins.
- Around **Day 48**, downsized warehouse operations so raw materials and machines matched moderate B2B demand.

### 📣 Marketing Mix (4Ps)

| P | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| **Product** | Full range of juices, prioritising high-demand items |
| **Price** | Profit-oriented pricing while staying competitive |
| **Promotion** | Multi-platform digital campaigns |
| **Place** | Expanded first in highest-demand locations, then added more |

## 💡 Key Lessons

1. **Cash discipline wins.** We finished with RM 116M in the bank, no overdraft used and zero delinquent payouts.
2. **Scale production to cut unit cost.** It was our answer to competitors undercutting on price.
3. **Fix bottlenecks fast.** Late B2B shipments peaked at 44, and adding machines and capacity brought them under control.
4. **Right-size when demand changes.** Downsizing the warehouse around Day 48 stabilised operating expenses.
5. **Prioritise high-margin deals** and buy inputs when prices are low.
6. **Diversify channels.** Retail and B2B each contributed roughly half of revenue.
